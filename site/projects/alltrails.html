Case Study / 01

AllTrails
Group Hike

Mobile App Feature Design UX Research
Role Primary Designer
Year 2023
Duration 4 Weeks
Tools Figma, Adobe Suite
Type Feature Design

Overview

01

AllTrails is the world's largest trail navigation app. It has over 65 million registered users as of 2024. None of them can coordinate a hike with another user inside the app.

This project adds a peer-to-peer Group Hike system to AllTrails' existing Community tab. Any user can host an event tied to a specific trail, set it public or private, and share a join code. Any user can browse nearby events and join with a single tap. The feature lives entirely inside AllTrails. It adds no new top-level navigation and disrupts no existing flow.

It was a solo school project, built over four weeks in 2023.

Problem Statement

02

AllTrails' social architecture is retrospective. You follow people. You see what they did after they did it. You tag them in an activity once you have already hiked together. There is no coordination primitive. No way to say "I am hiking this trail on Saturday, who is coming?"

A widely read product teardown flagged that AllTrails does not prompt users to connect a social graph during onboarding. That limits organic word-of-mouth growth. A separate analyst noted that AllTrails' user base skews toward casual and intermediate hikers. That group is the most likely to want company. The product offers no way to act on that preference.

The consequence is a visible escape route. Hikers who want to organise a group move to Meetup, WhatsApp, or Facebook Groups. Many of those posts link back to AllTrails for the trail data. AllTrails is the information layer. Other platforms own the coordination layer. This feature closes that gap.

Research & Analysis

03

Outdoor participation in the United States reached 175.8 million people in 2023. That is 57.3% of Americans aged six and above, per the Outdoor Industry Association's 2024 Participation Trends Report. Hiking was the single most popular activity at 20% participation. There is a critical detail underneath the headline. While participation is up, average session frequency dropped 11.4% from 2022. More people are hiking. They are just doing it less often.

Research also drew on App Store and Google Play reviews, along with posts from r/vancouver and outdoor hiking communities on Reddit. Three consistent themes surfaced. Vancouver users described the city as socially difficult to break into, making it hard to find hiking companions organically. Wildlife concerns came up repeatedly, with bears mentioned by name. Users felt more confident with others present. Fitness mismatch was the third frustration. Users wanted to hike with people at a similar pace rather than hold a group back or be left behind.

The behavioral case for group hiking is strong. Schnall, Harber, Stefanucci, and Proffitt (Journal of Experimental Social Psychology, 2008) showed that people standing at the base of a hill estimate its gradient as steeper when alone. With a friend present, the mean visual estimate dropped from 49.24 degrees to 44.07 degrees. The effect grew stronger the longer the friendship had lasted. The presence of another person literally changes how physically demanding a trail looks. A 2025 Frontiers in Public Health review concluded that group hiking builds trust and a sense of belonging through shared physical challenge. The NIH-funded GO-OUT randomized controlled trial found that community programs with structure, accountability, and social interaction measurably improved motivation to walk outdoors.

Every major competitor has a partial solution. None of them combine authoritative trail data with peer-to-peer event creation in a single product.

Gaia GPS Strong trail data, navigation, and offline maps. No social or group feature of any kind. The opposite end of the gap.
Meetup Peer event creation with RSVP and public or private settings. No trail data. Hikers end up in two apps and link back to AllTrails inside Meetup posts.

User Persona

04

Research converged on a clear primary user. A young professional, recently relocated to a new city, who hikes regularly but struggles to find company. She uses AllTrails to plan solo hikes but has to switch to Meetup or Facebook to find people to go with.

VR

Valentina Reynolds

Age 28  ·  Software Engineer  ·  Vancouver, BC

Goals & Needs

  • Meet people who share her pace and interest in moderate-difficulty trails
  • Organise or join a hike with minimal back-and-forth coordination
  • Feel safer on remote trails by hiking with a group

Pain Points

  • Limited social circle after moving to Vancouver six months ago
  • Work schedule makes organising anything across multiple apps exhausting
  • No way to filter Meetup hiking events by trail difficulty or fitness match

Goals

05
01

Social Coordination

Let any user create a real event tied to a real trail. Set a date and time, define group size, and share it without leaving AllTrails. The workflow should take under two minutes.

02

Safety by Design

Group hiking is empirically safer than solo hiking. Joining a group should be as frictionless as possible. Occupancy should be visible before a user commits so they know how full an event is.

03

Fit-Based Discovery

AllTrails already knows a trail's difficulty, distance, and estimated duration. Surface that data on the event card so users can self-select into the right group without a conversation.

User Flow

06

The flow splits cleanly at the Events tab into two paths. Host and Join. The Host path runs four sequential steps through a bottom-sheet wizard before reaching a success state. The Join path branches further, into public discovery or private code entry, before reaching a single-tap join confirmation. Both paths terminate at Saved Events.

Design Decisions

07

Six decisions shaped the architecture of this feature. Each has a clear rationale grounded in either user research or established UX principles.

The host creation flow runs across four discrete steps: date and activity, group size, privacy, and a review and confirm. This is staged disclosure. Each step shows only what the user needs at that point, preventing cognitive overload before commitment. Baymard research puts three to four steps in the optimal range for mobile task flows.

The wizard launches as a bottom sheet over the Community tab rather than navigating to a new screen. Nielsen Norman Group recommends bottom sheets for temporarily important tasks that need focused attention but should remain dismissible. The trail context stays visible above the sheet. The user stays anchored to the content they came from.

The most non-obvious decision was splitting the public and private paths at Step 3 into two distinct terminal states. The original design combined "Get a link" and "Post to community" on a single sharing screen. It felt simpler to build. It was wrong. A host who chose "private" was being shown community posting options that contradicted their stated intent. The two-path model gives each success state a precise job. The public success screen emphasises community reach. The private success screen emphasises the join code and direct-share channels.

The join code is a five-character alphanumeric string. It solves a trust problem. Public events are discoverable by anyone in the Community tab. Private events require a code, so attendance is limited to people the host has directly invited. Eventbrite, Discord, and Komoot all use invite codes as their gating mechanism for restricted-access events.

Each event card shows a live occupancy ratio, for example 3 of 10. That signals social proof. It also lets users self-select out of events that feel too full or too empty without asking the host. It removes the awkward outcome of joining an event only to find you are the only attendee.

Both host and join paths terminate at Saved Events, in a dedicated Events tab alongside Lists and Downloads. Upcoming and Past pills let users see the state of every event they are part of without re-navigating to the Community tab. That gives the feature a persistent home outside the feed.

Iterations

08

The most significant revision was on Step 1 of the host flow. Specifically the date, time, and activity inputs.

The original used free-text fields. Three problems surfaced straight away. Free-text date input generates formatting errors and requires validation logic. Free-text time input creates 12-hour versus 24-hour ambiguity. The activity dropdown had no visible selected state, so users could not confirm their choice was registered. There was also an asymmetric affordance. Only the time field had a chevron.

The iterated version replaced all three with native controls. A date pill with chevron opens a native date picker. A time pill with matching chevron opens a native time picker. A segmented control handles the activity choice with Hiking pre-selected in a clear filled state. Both pills now share an editability signal.

Nielsen's fourth heuristic, error prevention, guided this directly. Constraining the input format removes the class of error rather than catching and explaining it after the fact. The final version requires no validation logic and no error state handling for these three fields.

Visual Design

09

The style guide extends AllTrails' existing brand identity. The primary palette uses deep forest greens for surfaces: #142800 as the base and #2C5601 for interactive containers. A high-luminance tertiary green #5DE372 signals selection, completion, and system feedback. White handles body text on dark surfaces.

The typeface is Beatrice across heading, body, and label scales. It was chosen for its modern geometric character and legibility at small sizes on mobile.

Reflection

11

This project ran a complete UX process in four weeks. Secondary research, competitor analysis, persona definition, user flow architecture, iteration, and high-fidelity design. Every decision was grounded in a specific user need or a documented design principle. Not aesthetic preference.

The most useful constraint was the rule against new navigation. Fitting Group Hike into the existing Community tab forced discipline about what belonged in the feature and what did not. Route planning tools, post-event recap screens, and chat threads were deliberately excluded. The feature does one job and does it completely.

The heuristic evaluation that followed confirmed the core flows are structurally sound. It also named the clear next priority: error state coverage. The current design handles the happy path. It does not yet cover a full event, a cancelled event, an expired join code, or a network failure during event creation. That is the next design pass.
