Service Hub — Trusted Home Services for Your Neighbourhood

Overview

00

A location-aware mobile marketplace designed specifically for adults aged 65 and over — connecting them with vetted local professionals (plumbers, electricians, HVAC technicians, painters, handymen, and gardeners) through a warm, trust-forward iOS experience built around the way seniors actually make decisions: through trusted referrals, clear credentials, and direct human contact.

Service Hub removes every friction point in the traditional contractor-hiring process for an audience that existing platforms have consistently underserved. Discovery, vetting, scheduling, and follow-up all happen inside a single app — with generous tap targets, high-contrast type, plain-language copy, and structured inputs that eliminate the formatting errors that trip up less frequent smartphone users.

The design centres on two primary values: transparency and control. Every professional card surfaces rate, rating, and verification status before the user commits. Every booking state — pending, confirmed, completed, cancelled — is named explicitly and visible at all times. Nothing requires the user to infer, guess, or call to find out where they stand.

Design Principle: The app should feel like a trusted neighbour who already knows the right person for the job — not a search engine that makes you do the vetting yourself.

Problem Space

01

For adults aged 65 and over, hiring a home service professional is not a minor inconvenience — it is a process loaded with anxiety, vulnerability, and risk. Three interlocking problems define why existing solutions consistently fail this audience.

01

Word of Mouth Has No Fallback

Seniors rely almost exclusively on personal referrals to find contractors. When their immediate network can't recommend someone, the search stalls entirely — existing online directories are too complex to navigate, too difficult to evaluate, and too often populated with unverified listings that offer no meaningful trust signal.

02

The Phone-Based Process Creates Anxiety

The current process — calling multiple businesses, describing the problem verbally, negotiating availability in real time — is cognitively demanding and leaves no record. For seniors who struggle to recall verbal agreements or who rely on written confirmation to feel secure, the absence of a paper trail is a significant source of stress.

03

Seniors Are Disproportionately Targeted by Fraud

Older adults are the most frequent victims of contractor fraud — overcharging, unlicensed work, and phantom services are well-documented patterns. Without a reliable way to verify that a professional is insured, background-checked, and legitimately credentialed before inviting them into their home, the decision to hire anyone carries genuine personal risk.

The JTBD framing: When a 70-year-old needs a leaking tap fixed, they want to feel confident they've hired someone safe and reliable — and they want written confirmation the job is booked so they can stop worrying about it and not have to call to check.

Research & Users

02

Three senior users were recruited for moderated usability testing on a first-iteration prototype. Their sessions exposed the gap between what the team assumed would work and what actually did — and directly shaped the final design.

ZN

Zarina Nadir, 74 — iPhone user, app-familiar

Lived in Canada for 30 years, originally from Yemen. Constantly needs help around the house but relies purely on word of mouth. Independently motivated — wants to solve problems herself without asking family. Flagged that the initial interface lacked a welcoming introduction.

RB

Rozy Bharwani, 81 — iPhone owner, infrequent user

Has a part-time caretaker but still needs supplemental help. Typically delegates contractor searches to her daughter. A strong candidate for the product — if the interface doesn't intimidate her into handing the phone away. Responded positively to large tap targets and clear category labels.

MG

Margaret, 72 — Proficient iPhone user

Comfortable with iOS apps. Relies on recommendations from friends and family. Brought the most granular UI critique — her feedback on contrast, icon legibility, booking flow clarity, and the need for a direct call option was the most actionable data gathered.

Key Research Findings

Validated Hypotheses Invalidated Assumptions
  1. All three users confirmed immediate, genuine need — concept validation was unanimous.
  2. Bottom tab navigation was intuitive without instruction — the four-tab mental model mapped correctly.
  3. The Upcoming / Completed / Cancelled booking filter was praised as clear and immediately useful.
  4. Trust badges (ID Verified, Background Checked, Insured) on provider cards were identified as critical to willingness to book.
  1. The initial dark colour scheme felt cold and hard to read — users wanted warmth and approachability.
  2. Category label text was too small; multiple users flagged it as a legibility barrier.
  3. The booking form's typed date/time input caused errors — users needed a structured picker, not a text field.
  4. Messaging-only contact was insufficient — users wanted the option to call the professional directly.

Design Decisions

03

Every significant design decision in the final prototype maps to a specific constraint — trust-building, accessibility, or reduction of cognitive load at the moment it matters most.

01

Warm, Minimal Visual Language

The final palette uses a cream background (#FCF9F6) with a deep burnt-brown primary (#702B00) — warm without being childish, distinctive without being aggressive. Users in testing rejected the initial dark scheme as cold. The revised palette tests above WCAG AA contrast on all interactive elements and reads as approachable rather than clinical.

02

Single-Purpose Welcome Screen

The app opens with one sentence — "Trusted home services for your neighbourhood" — and two actions: Get Started and I already have an account. There is no carousel, no feature list, no branding splash. Users who needed an introduction to what the app does get it in under three seconds; returning users are never blocked by it.

03

Location-Anchored Home Screen

After granting location permission, the home screen displays the user's neighbourhood name ("Lincoln Square, New York") as a persistent anchor. This signals to users that results are genuinely local — not a generic national directory. A six-category grid (Plumber, Electrician, HVAC, Painter, Handyman, Gardening) gives immediate access to the most common service types; a Top-rated professionals rail below handles serendipitous discovery.

04

Trust-Dense Provider Cards

Every professional listing shows star rating, review count, years of experience, and hourly rate in a scannable four-column grid — then surfaces three verification badges (ID Verified, Background Checked, Insured) in a single row below. The individual profile page adds a free-text bio, recent review quotes, and a sticky Book Now CTA. The booking screen then surfaces a secondary "Call [Name]" option alongside Request Booking, so users who prefer the phone are never forced to commit to in-app messaging.

05

Two-Step Booking Flow

Scheduling is split across two screens to reduce cognitive load per step. Screen one presents a full calendar picker and a grid of available time slots — tapping a slot populates a "Selected" summary strip at the bottom before advancing. Screen two asks a single open-ended question: "What do you need help with?" with a placeholder that gives concrete examples (leaking tap, blocked drain, low water pressure). A note below the field sets expectations: "Your provider will review your request and send a cost estimate before confirming."

06

Explicit Booking States

Post-submission, the confirmation screen shows a full booking summary — service, professional, date, estimated rate — with a clearly labelled PENDING pill. The screen explains in plain language that the professional will review and confirm shortly, and provides a path to My Bookings. In the bookings list, CONFIRMED and PENDING statuses are badged on each card in contrasting colours so users never have to infer state from context.

07

Full Lifecycle Booking Management

My Bookings uses three tabs — Upcoming, Completed, Cancelled — so the booking history is always organised and auditable. Rescheduling keeps the original booking context visible at the top of the screen while the new date picker is presented below, preventing the user from losing their reference point. Cancellations surface a brief confirmation screen followed by a post-cancellation state with an undo action, reducing the cost of accidental taps.

08

Structured Post-Service Review

After a job is completed, users are prompted to rate the professional with a star picker and optionally write a review. Quick-tag chips (On time, Good value, Would rebook, Professional) make the review actionable in under five seconds for users who don't want to write prose — while still feeding the rating system that future users will depend on.

09

Empty State as Graceful Fallback

When no professionals are available in the user's area, the app surfaces a clear message with two recovery paths — Expand search area and Browse other categories — and a third escalation: "contact us — we'll find the right person for you." Users are never left in a dead end. The empty state is an experience, not an error.

User Flow

04

The primary flow covers the full end-to-end journey a new user takes from first launch through a confirmed booking. Secondary paths branch for returning users (sign in → home), users who encounter an empty state, and post-service actions (review, reschedule, cancel).

Visual Design

06

The visual language was built around two values: warmth and legibility. The background (#FCF9F6) and primary (#702B00) pairing creates a high-contrast, approachable palette that reads as domestic rather than clinical. All interactive elements meet WCAG AA contrast requirements. Typography is set at generous sizes throughout, with category labels and body copy scaled above typical mobile defaults to accommodate a broad age range.

High Fidelity Screens

07

Final Design

08

The final prototype addresses every critical and high-severity finding from testing. The core user journey — from first launch to confirmed booking — spans five clear screens with no dead ends and no ambiguous states.

→

Onboarding: Welcome → Sign Up → Location

A three-screen entry sequence: welcome screen with value proposition, account creation (name, email, password, optional phone), then a location permission prompt. The sign-up copy — "So we can match you with local professionals" — explains why each field matters, reducing abandonment.

→

Discovery: Home → Category → Profile

The home screen surfaces six category tiles and a top-rated professionals rail. Tapping a category reveals a ranked list of local professionals with full trust signals. Tapping a professional opens their profile with bio, reviews, and a Book Now CTA.

→

Booking: Date/Time → Job Description → Confirmation

A two-screen booking flow: calendar + time slot selection, then a free-text job description. The confirmation screen shows a full summary with PENDING status and plain-language explanation of next steps. No ambiguity about what happens after the user taps Confirm.

→

Management: Upcoming / Completed / Cancelled

My Bookings provides a full view of booking history across three tabs. Each upcoming card shows status, allows rescheduling (with original booking context visible), and supports cancellation with an undo option. Push notifications keep users informed of status changes without requiring them to open the app.

→

Post-Service: Review + Messages

Completed jobs prompt a review with star rating, free-text input, and quick-tag chips. The Messages tab keeps all in-app communication with professionals in one place. Users can also call at any time from the booking screen — messaging is an addition, not a replacement.

Reflection

09

The most important lesson from Service Hub is how quickly assumed accessibility collapses under real user contact. The initial prototype was clean and structurally sound — logical navigation, clear information hierarchy, contrast ratios that felt sufficient. Then three senior users sat down with it and revealed precisely where those assumptions broke.

Adequate contrast for a 28-year-old designer is not adequate for aging eyes in natural light. An icon that reads instantly to someone steeped in app conventions is opaque to a first-time user of an on-demand service. The category grid that felt self-evident in Figma was confusing in the hands of the people it was built for. These are not edge cases — they are the product working exactly as designed for the wrong audience.

The modality finding was the most instructive. The team defaulted to messaging as the primary communication channel — it was simpler to build and matched how the team personally communicates. All three users wanted to be able to call. Meeting users in their existing behaviour, rather than nudging them toward yours, is one of the most consistently underweighted decisions in product design. A "Call [Name]" button costs almost nothing to add. Its absence would have undermined the product's core promise of trust.

What held up was the architecture. The tab model was navigated without instruction. The booking management structure was praised. The trust badge system resonated immediately. And every single participant said, unprompted, that the product would be useful to them. That is a strong foundation — and the final prototype is built on top of knowing exactly which parts of the initial design needed to change.
