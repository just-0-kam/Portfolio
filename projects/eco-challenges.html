Overview

Eco Challenges and Indigenous People is a non-fictional illustrated book that explores several environmental issues through the perspectives of Indigenous communities. It offers a comprehensive look at the interplay between Indigenous peoples and the ecological challenges they face and address. The book is structured around four themes — Water, Fire, Forest, and Earth — each providing a nuanced perspective on ecological challenges and the important contributions Indigenous communities make to environmental sustainability and cultural heritage.

The Four Themes

Water Indigenous relationships with water systems, water rights, and conservation practices
Fire Traditional burning practices, wildfire crises, and Indigenous land stewardship
Forest Deforestation, biodiversity loss, and the role of Indigenous communities as forest guardians
Earth Soil degradation, land rights, and Indigenous agricultural knowledge and practices

Design Rationale

The design of Eco Challenges and Indigenous People reflects the project's mission through a considered, contemporary aesthetic. Typography draws inspiration from nature and Indigenous art — striking a balance between visual interest and legibility. Chapter and section layouts are structured to maintain clear hierarchy across a 120-page print run, while the use of full-bleed imagery immerses readers in each theme. The overall approach prioritises consistency and visual impact while keeping the content — and the communities it represents — at the centre.

Visual Design

The colour palette is rooted in the natural world — earthy greens, deep blacks, and muted naturals that reflect the book's ecological subject matter without competing with the photography. Two typefaces carry the typographic system: a serif for body text to support long-form reading, and a contrasting display face for headings and chapter openers.

Print Production

The final book runs to 120 pages across four chapters. Full-bleed photography is used throughout, with chapter openers designed as bold, typographic statements. The layout system was built in Adobe InDesign with a consistent grid to ensure coherence across all three thematic sections.

Reflection

The typesetting and consistent use of a modular grid were the strongest outcomes of this project — mood boards proved invaluable in anchoring the visual language of each theme early. Looking back, I would invest more time in the complexity and variety of individual page layouts, and source more powerful imagery to match the weight of the subject matter. The project reinforced the importance of typography as a design tool and deepened my understanding of designing with cultural sensitivity and context.
